shavar — SHA-256 as two coupled order-4 recurrences

The standard describes the compression function as eight registers that shuffle every round. Six of those eight assignments are pure copies. Drop them and what remains is two sequences, A and E, each looking back four steps — the form in spec/SPEC.md §3, and the thing this page lets you step through one round at a time. Everything runs locally in Safari; nothing is sent anywhere.

    Message and parameters

    A message is a byte buffer plus a bit count. When the bit count is not a multiple of 8, the final byte carries its significant bits in the high positions and its low bits must be zero — nonzero low bits are rejected, never silently masked, because masking would map two different inputs to one digest (SPEC.md §5.1).

    Fewer than 64 is not SHA-256; it is the reduced-round variant used for cryptanalysis.

    The two tracks

    Each cell is one 32-bit word: the top row is A[-4 … 63], the bottom row E[-4 … 63]. The four cells to the left of the current round are its lookback window. Arrows show dataflow into round t and are coloured by the track the data comes from. The bar under each value is that word's population count out of 32.

    Inside round 0 — every word, bit by bit

    Bit 31 (most significant) is on the left. Click any bit column to track that position down the whole round; click again to release it. Only carries in ⊞ move information between bit positions — every other operation here acts on each of the 32 positions independently (SPEC.md §7.2).

    Message schedule

    W[0…15] are the block's own words; after that W[t] = σ1(W[t-2]) ⊞ W[t-7] ⊞ σ0(W[t-15]) ⊞ W[t-16]. The four cells feeding the selected round are outlined. This recurrence is GF(2)-linear apart from its three additions, which is what message-modification attacks exploit (SPEC.md §4).

    Difference between message A and message B

    ΔX[t] = X[t] ⊕ X′[t]. The chart is the Hamming weight of each difference across the rounds — the standard first diagnostic for whether a differential path is behaving. A single flipped input bit should saturate near 16 of 32 within a dozen rounds; watching where it does not is the interesting part (SPEC.md §7.3). Click the chart to jump to a round.
